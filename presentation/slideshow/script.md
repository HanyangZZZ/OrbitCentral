## Slide 2: Problem & Opportunity (~30 s)

### Logic Flow

```
Problem Statement (the gap)
  → Users can't find what they need online (44% scroll 3+ min, 41% reformulate)
  → Local businesses can't reach customers (50%+ struggle, 60% say it's #1 challenge)
  → Digital-physical disconnect: both sides lose
        ↓
Market Need (the bridge)
  → 1.2 M+ Canadian small businesses fragmented across platforms
  → No single platform connects high-intent users to local businesses
  → Orbit bridges this gap
        ↓
Target Audience (why Gen Z)
  → 63% prefer in-store discovery
  → $450 B+ global spending power
  → Mobile-first, community-conscious
        ↓
Persona (make it concrete)
  → Sally Chen, 19, university student
  → Searches by vibe, not keywords
```

### Script

**[Problem — point to bridge visual, left cliff]**

"There's a growing digital-physical disconnect. As technology develops, users are increasingly searching online before buying — but **44 percent say it takes over 3 minutes of scrolling just to find what they need**, and **41 percent have to reformulate their searches** to get useful results. The tools aren't keeping up with the intent.

**[Point to right cliff]**

On the other side, local businesses are struggling just as much. **Over half say their biggest challenge is finding or retaining customers**, and **60 percent list acquiring new customers as their number-one marketing struggle**. These businesses need exposure, but existing platforms keep them fragmented and invisible.

**[Point to bridge deck + caption]**

This is the gap — and it's exactly the market need our platform addresses. We built Orbit to be the bridge: one AI-powered platform that connects high-intent digital users directly to local businesses. Looking at Canada alone, over 1.2 million employer businesses span retail, food, health care, and personal services — all scattered across Google Maps, Yelp, and word of mouth. Orbit ties them together.

**[Why Gen Z — point to stat cards]**

So who is this for? We chose **Gen Z** as our target audience — and the data backs it. **63 percent of Gen Z prefer in-store discovery** over online shopping. They *want* to visit local spots. And collectively, they hold over **450 billion dollars in direct spending power** globally. They have the intent and the means — they just need the right platform.

**[Persona — point to persona card]**

Meet Sally — a 19-year-old university student here in Markham. She's mobile-first, researches everything on her phone, but prefers buying in person at local spots. Her frustration? Google Maps shows the nearest, not the best — and she wants to search by *vibe*, not just star ratings. Sally represents millions of Gen Z users waiting for a smarter local discovery experience.

That's the gap. That's the opportunity. And that's what Orbit solves."

## Slide 3: Our Solution (~15 s)

**[Point to logo and title]**

"So how do we solve this? Meet **Orbit** — the best spots gravitate towards you.

**[Point to 'How We Solve the Problem']**

The core of our solution is **AI hyper-personalization**. Orbit learns each user's taste from their reviews and bookmarks, then surfaces the most relevant nearby spots — so local businesses actually reach the people who'd love them most.

**[Point to USP]**

Our unique selling proposition is simple: we are not just another review app — we are a **service**. Orbit is the only local discovery platform that makes the best nearby spots gravitate to students through hyper-personalized recommendations, powered by AI that learns your taste — not just your keywords.

**[Point to brand positioning quadrant chart]**

And here's where we stand relative to competitors. On the x-axis we have personalization, on the y-axis community focus. Google Maps and TripAdvisor sit in the lower-left — generic discovery, low on both. Yelp has community but limited personalization. Instagram is algorithm-driven but lacks local discovery structure. Orbit is the only platform occupying the **top-right quadrant** — high personalization and high community focus. That's the gap we fill, and no one else does."

## Slide 4: Software Concept (~20 s)

### Logic Flow

```
Concept (what Orbit is)
  → Dynamic, context-aware ecosystem — not static search-and-list
        ↓
Analogy (the user as the star)
  → User at the center, businesses orbit around them
  → Relevance determined by taste, proximity, time
        ↓
Three Highlights (what makes us stand out)
  → Vibe Search: natural-language queries → semantic ranking
  → Hyper-Personalization: RAG pipeline → tailored feed
  → AI Review System: conversational writing → enriched vibe data
        ↓
Student Involvement (how features accomplish the task)
  → Vibe Search + Personalization + AI Reviews
  → = effortless local community engagement
```

### Script

**[Point to lead text]**

"The concept behind Orbit is simple: we shift from a static search-and-list model to a **dynamic, context-aware ecosystem**.

**[Point to orbit animation]**

Think of it like this — the user is the **star**, and local businesses orbit around them. Cafés, restaurants, salons — they all gravitate towards you based on what's most relevant.

**[Gesture across all three cards]**

Three features make this work. **Vibe Search** — type natural language, and results are ranked by semantic similarity, proximity, and rating — search by vibe, not keywords. **Hyper-Personalization** — a RAG pipeline retrieves your real reviews and bookmarks, then GPT-4.1 distills them into a taste-capturing query, so every recommendation is uniquely yours. **AI Review System** — chat post-visit, and GPT auto-tags businesses via function calling — effortless reviews that enrich discovery for everyone.

**[Point to student involvement flow]**

Together, these three drive **student involvement**: find spots that match your mood, let AI learn your interests, share experiences effortlessly — and the result is effortless local community engagement."
