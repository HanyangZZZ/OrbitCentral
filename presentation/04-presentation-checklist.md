# Presentation Checklist

> **Source:** [04-presentation-checklist-1.png](04-presentation-checklist-1.png), [05-presentation-checklist-2.png](05-presentation-checklist-2.png)

---

## 1. Introduction

Give a brief introduction on the concept of the software and your process in designing and building it.

- What connects your program to the problem statement?
- How does your program's concept best accomplish the task of helping student involvement?
- What is the name, brand, and Intellectual Property surrounding the supposed brand you've built?

---

## 2. Proof-of-Concept

Your project will be graded, first and foremost, on the extent of the criteria that you are able to meet.

- Prepare an **intuitive, easily understandable method** of introducing every feature you have coded.
- **General to Specific Description:** the order of presentation must not be random or impulsive. Taking a general-to-specific approach to the screens/pages of your software helps keep your presentation understandable.
- **Intuitive Software:** it is of utmost importance to clearly and intuitively organize all the features that you've incorporated. UI and frontend work is also very important to scoring, as they contribute to the professionalism that you and your program entail.
- In explaining each feature that you implemented, be sure to also explain the **necessity or benefits** from its presence.

---

## 3. Demonstrations

Demonstrating the efficacy of your program is vital to your presentation.

- **Multi-user interfaces:** Some features might require multiple users to become effective. In the case that these types of features show up in your program, develop a creative way to showcase the feature, regardless of external requirements. Keep in mind that no additional or external aid from judges is permitted.
- **Bug testing:** Bugs should be accounted for prior to the presentation. For the most effective presentation, no bugs should be present. Consider demonstrating short attempts of breaking the code to display **error trapping**.
- **Walk-through Style:** Demonstrate the various features of your program as though teaching it to users or clients. If there are multiple interfaces or portals, be sure to make the distinction between types of users.
- **Order of Importance:** Demonstrate each feature in order of descending importance and priority. If you have many features to show, keep in mind that you have to ration out presentation time to each feature very carefully. Despite the short amount of time you have, be absolutely sure that every aspect of your page is still being mentioned.

---

## 4. Conclusion

With your remaining time:

- Acknowledge the various **external software packages** that you've incorporated into your program.
- Mention the **languages and coding software** that each segment of your program was created with.
- Very briefly **summarize** what you've done in the presentation.
- **Thank your judge** for listening.
